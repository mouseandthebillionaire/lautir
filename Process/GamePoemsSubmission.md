## Bio

M Bethancourt develops experimental games and interactive works about contemplative play, ritual, attention, procedural sound, and the strange pressure of everyday systems. His work often asks players to do simple or mundane things that open onto larger questions of time, perception, and meaning. He holds an MFA in Design and Technology from Parsons School of Design in New York and is currently a PhD candidate at Concordia University in Montreal, where his research explores contemplative play through art/design practice.

## Intent And Thematic Connection

LAUTIR is a small ritual game about waiting for a narrow daily window, entering a word, and listening as that word becomes part of a short procedural song. For the "Videogames Inside" theme, the piece treats familiar videogame materials--timing windows, input, state, repetition, and procedural feedback--as poetic devices for attention rather than progress.

## Tools And Process

LAUTIR is built in Unity for WebGL, with procedural sound composed and generated through Max/RNBO. The process moved through timed-access prototypes, visual alignment studies, and word-to-music sketches before settling on a five-word song structure shaped by repeated daily input.

## Artist Statement

LAUTIR began as a question about what happens when a game refuses constant availability. The work gives players a short daily window in which to enter a word, then slowly converts those words into a layered procedural song. Its core action is simple, but the surrounding structure asks the player to wait, return, notice timing, and let a tiny interaction gather weight across days.

The piece uses videogame conventions--time gates, typed input, stored state, procedural systems, and browser constraints--as poetic material. Rather than making progress visible through score or completion, LAUTIR turns small acts of alignment into an attentional ritual: being there at the right moment, choosing a word, and listening to what the system makes from it.
